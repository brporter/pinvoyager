Sign in

Four buttons and nothing else. There is no email field, no password field, and no registration screen — a successful first sign-in is the signup.

The absence of a signup screen is the design. Authentication is delegated entirely and no credential is ever stored, so there is nothing to register. The heading says "Sign in or create an account" precisely because a first-time visitor needs to know this one screen serves them too — without that, people hunt for a "Sign up" link that does not exist.

Annotations

1"We never see or store a password" is a genuine property of this architecture, not marketing. It is worth saying, because a user landing on four unfamiliar buttons wants to know why there is no password field.
2"Every voyage you create is public" appears before sign-in, not after. It is the single most surprising property of the product and the one most likely to cause regret, so it belongs at the gate.
3Uniform buttons, provider-mandated skins. Shared height, radius, icon size, and label grammar, so this reads as four equivalent choices rather than four vendors competing.
4No "remember me" checkbox. Sessions are server-side and long-lived by default; a checkbox implying otherwise would be theatre.
