Upload flow

Three steps: choose files, watch them process, review where they landed. The review step exists because EXIF is unreliable, and a user needs to see what was actually derived before it becomes public.

Your originals are never published. PinVoyager reads the location and time from each photo, then serves re-encoded copies with all metadata removed.

The stripping guarantee appears at the point of upload as well as at the granularity control. This is the moment a user is deciding whether to trust the product with their camera roll, so it is the moment the promise is worth the space.

Annotations

1The review map shows where the photos actually landed before anything is published. EXIF coordinates can be wrong — a camera with a stale GPS fix will place a photo hundreds of kilometres away — and this is the only chance to notice before it is public.
2Unplaced photos get an explicit decision, not a silent default. The user is told what will happen and can opt out.
3"Leave in my library" is a real option. Uploading and assigning are separate acts, and forcing every upload into a voyage immediately would make the library concept meaningless.
4The primary button counts what will actually happen — "Add 4 photos", not "Done". Six were selected; four are being added.
