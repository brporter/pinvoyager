Account settings

Profile, default granularity, linked providers, and deletion. There is no password section, no two-factor section, and no email preferences — none of those exist in this architecture.

Unlinking the last provider must be refused. With social-only sign-in and no stored credentials, a user who removes their only identity has no way back into the account — and no support path we control can restore it, because there is nothing to prove ownership against. The last provider's unlink control is disabled with an explanation.

Annotations

1The disabled Unlink button is paired with an explanation. A disabled control with no reason attached reads as a bug; here the warning states the consequence and the remedy.
2Linking is prompted, not just permitted. Account recovery is genuinely impossible in this architecture, so the interface nudges toward a second provider rather than waiting for the user to work that out.
3No email address is shown anywhere on this screen. Whether to store provider-supplied email at all is an open question in phase 3, and the recommendation is not to — it is not needed for sign-in and creates PII we would otherwise not hold.
4Deletion says what happens to published voyages. Other people may be linking to them; the user should know those links break.
