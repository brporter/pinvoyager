Voyage detail

The core screen, and the only genuinely hard layout in the product: a map and a photo list both want to be the primary object, and on a phone they cannot both be.

The two treatments are different layouts, not one layout reflowed. Desktop puts map and photos side by side, because there is room for both to be primary. Mobile makes the map the surface and the photos a sheet drawn over it, because 390px cannot host two primary objects — a stacked map-above-list would give each half a screen and make both useless.

Annotations

1Selection is bidirectional. The selected pin and the selected photo are highlighted simultaneously. Clicking either one selects the other — clicking a pin scrolls its photo into view in the rail; selecting a photo pans the map and marks its pin. This is the interaction the whole screen exists for.
2The granularity badge sits in the header. A viewer needs to know that "~1 km" is the precision they are looking at, or they will read the pins as exact.
3Photos are grouped by capture date. Chronology is the only ordering that needs no explanation for a travelogue.
4Unplaced photos are visible at the bottom of the rail, not omitted. A user who uploaded 54 photos and sees 48 on the map needs to be told where the other six went.

Annotations

1The sheet has two rest positions — peek (168px, showing a filmstrip) and expanded (~300px, showing the selected photo plus the strip). Selecting a pin expands it; the grip drags it back down.
2Attribution moves with the sheet. OSM attribution is a license requirement, so it cannot be allowed to slide underneath the sheet and disappear.
3Gesture ownership is the main risk here. The sheet sets touch-action: pan-y so a vertical drag scrolls the sheet rather than panning the map underneath. Getting this wrong makes the map lurch whenever someone scrolls the photo list, and it is the defect most likely to survive to production because it does not reproduce with a mouse.
4The top bar floats over the map rather than occupying a band above it, so the map keeps the full height of a small screen.

What phase 7 must preserve

The map here is a static stand-in. When MapLibre replaces it, the pin geometry (26px visible, 44px hit area), the cluster tiers, the area rendering for coarse granularity, the attribution placement, and both directions of selection all have to survive the swap. The layout is the contract; the renderer is the implementation.
