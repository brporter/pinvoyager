Granularity selector

The one genuinely novel control in this product, and the most consequential. Every voyage is public, so this setting is the only thing standing between an EXIF coordinate and the world.

This control has to make the consequence legible before publishing, not after. A dropdown reading "~10 km" tells someone nothing about what they are actually revealing. The live area preview does: it shows the region a viewer would be able to infer, at the scale they would see it.

The four steps

Exact

© OpenStreetMap contributors

Photos appear at the coordinates recorded by your camera. Anyone can see the precise spot each was taken.

~1 km

© OpenStreetMap contributors

Photos appear somewhere within about a kilometre — enough to show the neighbourhood, not the address.

~10 km

© OpenStreetMap contributors

Photos appear within about ten kilometres — roughly the town or district, not the street.

~50 km

© OpenStreetMap contributors

Photos appear within about fifty kilometres — the region only.

The stripping guarantee

Without this, the whole control is decorative. If a user selects ~50 km but the served JPEG still carries its original GPS EXIF, anyone who downloads the image reads the exact coordinates straight out of the file. Re-encoding derived variants with all metadata stripped is what makes the setting mean anything — so the promise is stated here, where the choice is made, rather than buried in a policy page.
Location data is removed from the files themselves. Images served by PinVoyager are re-encoded with all EXIF metadata stripped — including GPS, timestamp, and camera model. Your original upload is never served.

Open questions this control raises

Rounding must be stable. Reading the same photo twice has to return identical coordinates. If the coarsened position is re-sampled per request, pins jitter visibly between page loads — and worse, repeated observation lets someone average the samples back toward the true location, which defeats the entire feature.

Grid rounding versus fixed offset. Rounding to a grid is simple and deterministic, but clusters every pin onto grid intersections, which looks artificial. A stable per-photo offset derived from the photo ID looks natural but needs care that it cannot be averaged away. Recorded as an open question in phase 5.

Per voyage or per photo? Drawn here as a per-voyage setting with a user-level default. Per-photo control is more flexible and considerably more interface — and it raises the question of what a voyage-level indicator would even mean when its photos disagree.
