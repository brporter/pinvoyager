Selection changes three things at once: hue, scale, and halo. Hue alone would fail for a meaningful number of users, and on a map the marker is often sitting on an arbitrary background where a single-signal change is easy to miss even with full color vision.

Hit area

The visible marker is 26px. The button around it is 44px, outlined below. Dense pin clusters on a phone are unusable otherwise — the tap lands between two markers and nothing happens, which reads as the app being broken rather than the finger being imprecise.

Granularity areas

A coarse voyage draws an area, not a point. Rendering a sharp pin at the center of a 10km rounding makes a precision claim the data cannot support — and it visually undoes the choice the user deliberately made. The circle's radius reflects the actual precision, so what is drawn is what is known.

Attribution and controls

OpenStreetMap attribution is required by the ODbL license under which the map data is published. It is not a courtesy or a nicety — it appears on every map instance, and removing it is a license violation.

Zoom controls stay on screen even though pinch-zoom works, because pointer and keyboard users need a non-gesture path to the same capability. Both controls are 44px.

In dark mode the pin lightens to pin-500 and its stroke inverts to near-black. A dark-outlined marker on a light map and a light-outlined marker on a dark map are both legible; keeping one stroke color for both is not.

Notes for phase 7

MapLibre animates camera movement internally, outside CSS, so the token-level reduced-motion collapse does not reach it. The preference has to be read and passed into the map's own animation options, or camera flights keep animating for users who asked them not to.

The sheet on mobile sets touch-action: pan-y so that a vertical drag on the sheet does not also pan the map underneath it. Getting this wrong produces a map that lurches whenever someone tries to scroll the photo list, and it is the most likely gesture defect on the voyage detail screen.
